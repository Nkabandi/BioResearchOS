What the findings mean

Discussion

  • Resistance to first-line antibiotics is effectively the norm in East African bloodstream isolates, leaving second-line agents exposed.
  • The wide NAP implementation range shows surveillance quality varies widely, which can understate true prevalence.
  • ESBL and MRSA ranges are broad, reflecting heterogeneous studies and settings more than true uncertainty.
  • Surveillance systems are strongest where they sample consistently and publish openly.

What changes because of these findings

Decision Implications

  • If a country under-reports NAP progress, then regional AMR trend estimates are biased and resources misallocated.
  • If ceftriaxone resistance is rising, then empiric sepsis protocols relying on it need stewardship review.
  • If community ESBLs are spreading, then hospital and community surveillance both warrant expansion.

What the findings mean

Discussion

  • ART-R marker prevalence of ~5% is still low, but the trajectory matters: it mirrors Southeast Asia's earlier timeline.
  • The log-OR -2.06 association with treatment failure shows markers are clinically meaningful, not just molecular curiosities.
  • Partner-drug tolerance (lumefantrine) is emerging while ACTs remain effective overall - the vulnerable window is now.
  • HRP2/3 deletions at these levels would degrade RDT-only diagnosis strategies.

What changes because of these findings

Decision Implications

  • If surveillance is not expanded now, then marker spread will be detected only after treatment failure rises.
  • If RDT reliance continues without HRP2/3 deletion monitoring, then false-negative diagnosis risk grows.
  • If partner-drug tolerance spreads, then ACT policy adaptation will be needed within a treatment window that is closing.

CRISPR-based TB diagnostics have reached clinically viable performance for point-of-care use: lab-in-tube RPA+CRISPR-Cas12a detects MTB with 81% sensitivity (14% above GeneXpert) and 94% specificity in under an hour, meeting WHO TPP for non-sputum TB.

CRISPR-based TB diagnostics have reached clinically viable performance for point-of-care use: lab-in-tube RPA+CRISPR-Cas12a detects MTB with 81% sensitivity (14% above GeneXpert) and 94% specificity in under an hour, meeting WHO TPP for non-sputum TB.

cfDNA-based systems go further: CRISPR-Cas13 approaches reach 96% sensitivity / 94% specificity in adults and 100% in CLHIV.

Sources reviewed6Crossref-verified6/6Report dateAugust 2026

Read this first

Key Takeaways

  • Lab-in-tube RPA+CRISPR-Cas12a: 81% sensitivity vs culture (GeneXpert 68%), 94% specificity, <1h, meets WHO TPP for non-sputum TB (Sci Transl Med 2025).
  • CRISPR-TB cfDNA: 96%/94% sensitivity/specificity adults
  • 83%/95% children
  • 100% in CLHIV
  • signal clears by 6 months of treatment (Lancet Microbe 2022).
  • WATSON pooled genomic tiling + CRISPR/Cas13 detects Mtb-cfDNA with higher sensitivity than singleton targets
  • lateral-flow compatible (Nat Commun 2023).
  • RPA/CRISPR-Cas12f1 dual-set: LOD 10 copies/µL (fluorescence), 100 copies/µL (lateral flow)
  • 94.5-91.4% vs qPCR, 100% specificity, <1h (Microbiol Spectr 2025).
  • CADEM: species-level MTB/MAC/MAB cfDNA differentiation, no false positives, ~2h workflow (Anal Chim Acta 2026).

Why this report exists

Context & Objective

  • Conventional microscopy, X-ray and culture are slow or insensitive
  • point-of-care molecular options are limited.
  • CRISPR-based approaches (RPA-Cas12, Cas12f1, Cas13 tiling) promise rapid, specific detection from non-sputum samples.
  • Recent assays target both cellular RNA and circulating cell-free DNA (cfDNA) for TB.
  • WHO target product profile for non-sputum TB diagnostic is met by several recent systems.

Executive Summary

Cassava mosaic disease is advancing eastward across West Africa: the pandemic EACMV-Ug strain is now confirmed in Guinea, Sierra Leone and Côte d'Ivoire, with higher infection along the Liberia border (28.85%) than the Guinea border (17.07%), and every cultivated accession tested is susceptible.

Cassava mosaic disease is advancing eastward across West Africa: the pandemic EACMV-Ug strain is now confirmed in Guinea, Sierra Leone and Côte d'Ivoire, with higher infection along the Liberia border (28.85%) than the Guinea border (17.07%), and every cultivated accession tested is susceptible.

Whitefly-mediated transmission is rising (50.6% of spread in 2023), while improved varieties reduce CMD incidence by roughly 30 percentage points - surveillance and clean planting material are the binding constraints.

What the findings mean

Discussion

  • The West African front mirrors the dynamics that drove the 1990s East African pandemic - same strain, same vector route, same susceptibility landscape.
  • Improved varieties are the clearest protective lever, but distribution of clean cuttings lags awareness.
  • Whitefly share rising to ~50% means vector monitoring must sit alongside variety deployment.
  • Survey standardisation is the prerequisite for tracking the front across borders.

What changes because of these findings

Decision Implications

  • If clean, improved planting material is not scaled in the border zones, then the eastward front will continue through susceptible local varieties.
  • If whitefly-mediated transmission grows, then phytosanitation and vector monitoring become as important as variety choice.
  • If surveys stay non-standardised, then cross-border tracking of the front remains unreliable.

Immediate · Near-term · Long-term

Recommendations

Immediate

Deploy standardised incidence/severity surveys along the Liberia-Côte d'Ivoire border corridor.|NEAR|Scale distribution of improved, CMD-tolerant varieties with clean planting material in the front zones.|NEAR|Monitor whitefly populations and transmission share alongside CMD surveillance.|LONG|Coordinate regional seed-system and phytosanitary response across West African borders.
