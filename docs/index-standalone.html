01 — Executive Summary

BIORESEARCH·OS

Research at a glance

AMR-NAP implementation ranges 7% (Burundi) to 94% (Kenya)

WHO surveillance checklist completion 44-100%. Resistance to first-line drugs is 50-100% (ampicillin, cotrimoxazole) with emerging ceftriaxone resistance 46-69% in Gram-negatives. First community-acquired ESBL bloodstream infections reported in Zanzibar

MRSA 3.9-56.8% and ESBL 1.9-53% in healthcare-associated infections.

Research question

“What is the current state of AMR surveillance and bloodstream infection pathogen resistance in East Africa?”

02 — Key Findings

4 verified of 4 screened

What the evidence says

Finding 01

Progress on sustainable AMR-NAP implementation ranges 7% (Burundi) to 94% (Kenya); WHO AMR-surveillance checklist completion 44-100%; gaps in lab capacity, representativeness, integration and financing

Evidence EAC national public health labs / expert panel (Burundi, Kenya, Rwanda, South Sudan, Tanzania, Uganda)

Finding 02

High resistance to first-line drugs 50-100% (ampicillin, cotrimoxazole), emerging ceftriaxone resistance (46-69%) in Gram-negatives, mainly Klebsiella and E. coli; MRSA 2.6-4%

Evidence 12 East African studies (2005+, PubMed + AJOL), mostly bloodstream infections

Finding 03

Pathogenic bacteria in 14% of patients; first report of community-acquired ESBL Enterobacteriaceae bloodstream infections in Zanzibar; 6/7 Salmonella Typhi isolates multidrug resistant

Evidence 469 consecutively enrolled patients, Mnazi Mmoja Hospital, Zanzibar

01 — Executive Summary

BIORESEARCH·OS

Research at a glance

Pooled non-synonymous PfKelch13 prevalence is 5.0% (95% CI 3.0-7.0%) in East Africa

R561H and A675V are the most common validated markers

R622I is emerging in central Ethiopia (1.1%)

HRP2/HRP3 gene deletions are common (HRP2 del 24%, HRP3 del 79%). ART-R in Africa follows the Southeast Asian pattern 10-15 years earlier

clinical efficacy monitoring of ACTs is required.

Research question

“What molecular evidence supports the ongoing spread of artemisinin partial resistance in East Africa Plasmodium falciparum?”

02 — Key Findings

7 verified of 7 screened

What the evidence says

Finding 01

ART-R has emerged in multiple East African countries and is now a priority; recommended expansion of genomic surveillance, clinical efficacy trials, ex-vivo susceptibility testing, policy adaptation

Evidence Multi-institution review incl. WHO (Rossert HQ colleagues, Uganda/Tanzania/Rwanda)

Finding 02

7.0% non-synonymous Pfk13 mutations incl. WHO-validated R561H (2 patients, 2022); pfmdr1 N86 invariable; mean MOI 1.5; suggests emerging lumefantrine tolerance

Evidence 173 P. falciparum-positive children, Mwanza, Tanzania (2016-2022), 143 Pfk13 sequenced

Finding 03

Pooled non-synonymous PfKelch13 prevalence 5.0% (Cl 3.0-7.0%); R561H and A675V most common; mutations associated with higher risk of treatment failure (log OR -2.06)

Evidence Systematic review + meta-analysis of 24 studies (2014-2024), East Africa

CRISPR-Based Point-of-Care Diagnostics for Tuberculosis

01 — Executive Summary

BIORESEARCH·OS

Research at a glance

A portable lab-in-tube RPA-CRISPR-Cas12a assay detected TB with 81% sensitivity vs culture (GeneXpert 68%) and 94% specificity within 1 h, meeting the WHO target product profile for non-sputum TB. Detection of circulating cfDNA: 96%/94% in adults, 83%/95% in children, 100% in children living with HIV. WATSON (Cas13 tiled) and CAD13 species-level detection demonstrated feasibility.

Research question

“Can CRISPR/Cas molecular assays meet WHO performance targets for non-sputum TB diagnosis?”

02 — Key Findings

6 verified of 6 screened

What the evidence says

Finding 01

Portable lab-in-tube RPA+CRISPR-Cas12a assay detected MTB DNA with 81% sensitivity vs culture (68% GeneXpert) and 94% specificity, meeting WHO TPP for non-sputum TB; result within 1 h

Evidence Children 1-16y (serum cfDNA) + adults (sputum/saliva)

Finding 02

CRISPR-TB: 96% sensitivity / 94% specificity adults; 83%/95% children; 100% in CLHIV; seropositivity positively hazard; signal cleared by 6 months of treatment

Evidence Adults, children incl. HIV-positive, Eswatini + Kenya (archived serum)

Finding 03

WATSON (pooled genomic tiling + CRISPR/Cas13) detects Mtb-cfDNA with higher sensitivity than singleton target for whole-genome, and compatible with lateral flow tradu in future

Evidence Plasma from active pulmonary TB patients (proof-of-concept)

01 — Executive Summary

BIORESEARCH·OS

Research at a glance

Data from 138 low- and middle-income countries shows public-sector R&D spending, not private-sector, is the key driver of policy framings around biotech. FAO/WHO food-safety risk assessments and Codex guidance on LMIC contexts show converging approaches to regulator trust. Ghanaian studies demonstrate farmers' 85.8% willingness-to-pay and 81-89% consumer acceptance

digital-agriculture reviews highlight access-equity regulation for smallholder inclusive adoption.

Research question

“What evidence describes public acceptance, policy support, and adoption of biotech crops in low-income agricultural settings?”

02 — Key Findings

7 verified of 7 screened

What the evidence says

Finding 01

EACMV-Ug (Uganda 1990s pandemic strain) detected across Guinea and Sierra Leone; spread via infected cuttings; all 63 cultivated accessions susceptible; established eastward front developing in West Africa

Evidence country-wide survey, Guinea April 2024; + 2022 Kambia (Sierra Leone) samples

Finding 02

Standardised survey methods required to map the ongoing CMD pandemic, because whitefly (Bemisia tabaci) dynamics govern epidemic spread

Evidence Review of field survey methods, whitefly IPM across Africa

Finding 03

Separateness and superabundant whitefly populations drive CMG/CBSV pandemic evolution; southern diversity and complex interactions need integrated surveillance

Evidence Review of whitefly-transmitted viruses (CMG & CBSV), endosymbiont work
