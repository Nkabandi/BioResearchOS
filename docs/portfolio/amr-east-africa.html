What the findings mean

Discussion

  • Resistance to first-line antibiotics is effectively the norm in East African bloodstream isolates, leaving second-line agents exposed.
  • The wide NAP implementation range shows surveillance quality varies widely, which can understate true prevalence.
  • ESBL and MRSA ranges are broad, reflecting heterogeneous studies and settings more than true uncertainty.
  • Surveillance systems are strongest where they sample consistently and publish openly.

What changes because of these findings

Decision Implications

  • If a country under-reports NAP progress, then regional AMR trend estimates are biased and resources misallocated.
  • If ceftriaxone resistance is rising, then empiric sepsis protocols relying on it need stewardship review.
  • If community ESBLs are spreading, then hospital and community surveillance both warrant expansion.

Immediate · Near-term · Long-term

Recommendations

Immediate

Improve laboratory capacity and core surveillance representativeness in the weakest EAC states.|NEAR|Standardize regional AMR national reporting and align with the WHO surveillance checklist.|NEAR|Secure sustainable financing for national reference laboratories.|LONG|Link clinical data to national surveillance to catch drug-resistance shifts earlier.

Stated plainly

Limitations

Only publicly available literature was analyzed. Findings reflect only the papers screened; absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence. This report verifies citation provenance, not the validity of the underlying studies. Some studies used heterogeneous methodologies, limiting direct comparability. Clinical validation was not performed. It is intended for research and is not a medical device.
