Bottom Line Up Front

Executive Summary

CRISPR-based TB diagnostics have reached clinically viable performance for point-of-care use: lab-in-tube RPA+CRISPR-Cas12a detects MTB with 81% sensitivity (14% above GeneXpert) and 94% specificity in under an hour, meeting WHO TPP for non-sputum TB.

cfDNA-based systems go further: CRISPR-Cas13 approaches reach 96% sensitivity / 94% specificity in adults and 100% in CLHIV.

Key Takeaways

  • Lab-in-tube RPA+CRISPR-Cas12a: 81% sensitivity vs culture (GeneXpert 68%), 94% specificity, <1h, meets WHO TPP for non-sputum TB (Sci Transl Med 2025).
  • CRISPR-TB cfDNA: 96%/94% sensitivity/specificity adults
  • 83%/95% children
  • 100% in CLHIV
  • signal clears by 6 months of treatment (Lancet Microbe 2022).
  • WATSON pooled genomic tiling + CRISPR/Cas13 detects Mtb-cfDNA with higher sensitivity than singleton targets
  • lateral-flow compatible (Nat Commun 2023).
  • RPA/CRISPR-Cas12f1 dual-set: LOD 10 copies/µL (fluorescence), 100 copies/µL (lateral flow)
  • 94.5-91.4% vs qPCR, 100% specificity, <1h (Microbiol Spectr 2025).
  • CADEM: species-level MTB/MAC/MAB cfDNA differentiation, no false positives, ~2h workflow (Anal Chim Acta 2026).

Why this report exists

Context & Objective

  • Conventional microscopy, X-ray and culture are slow or insensitive
  • point-of-care molecular options are limited.
  • CRISPR-based approaches (RPA-Cas12, Cas12f1, Cas13 tiling) promise rapid, specific detection from non-sputum samples.
  • Recent assays target both cellular RNA and circulating cell-free DNA (cfDNA) for TB.
  • WHO target product profile for non-sputum TB diagnostic is met by several recent systems.

Research question

“How do CRISPR-based diagnostic assays for tuberculosis perform at the point of care, and do they meet WHO target product profiles?”

In scope

  • CRISPR-based TB detection
  • laboratory-in-tube and lateral-flow platforms
  • sputum and non-sputum (blood/urine) samples
  • 2022-2026 literature

Out of scope

  • Sputum smear microscopy
  • culture-based workflows
  • drug-resistance genotyping
  • pharmacoeconomics
  • regulatory submissions

What the findings mean

Discussion

  • The strongest near-term win is non-sputum testing: molecular diagnosis from cell-free DNA or blood bypasses specimen collection barriers.
  • Performance is comparable to or better than existing rapid molecular tests in the head-to-head comparisons reported.
  • The movement toward lateral-flow readability addresses the biggest deployment constraint in low-resource settings.
  • Species-level differentiation (CADEM) hints at multi-pathogen panels on one platform.

What changes because of these findings

Decision Implications

  • If non-sputum CRISPR tests are validated at scale, then sample collection and transport bottlenecks to TB diagnosis shrink.
  • If lateral-flow-readable CRISPR tests reach market, then true community-level POC diagnosis becomes realistic.
  • If cfDNA signal clears with treatment, then the same assay can double as treatment monitoring.

Immediate · Near-term · Long-term

Recommendations

Immediate

Validate lead CRISPR platforms against culture in high-burden African sites.|NEAR|Field-test non-sputum (blood-based) workflows and lateral-flow usability in clinic settings.|NEAR|Integrate species-level differentiation (MTB vs MAC/MAB) in panels for CFU-ambiguous patients.|LONG|Pursue WHO endorsement and registry/regulatory pathways with package-design partners.

Limitations

Only publicly available literature was analyzed. Findings reflect only the papers screened; absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence. This report verifies citation provenance, not the validity of the underlying studies. Some studies used heterogeneous methodologies, limiting direct comparability. Clinical validation was not performed. It is intended for research and is not a medical device.
