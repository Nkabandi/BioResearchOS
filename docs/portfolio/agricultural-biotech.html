Bottom Line Up Front

Executive Summary

Cassava mosaic disease is advancing eastward across West Africa: the pandemic EACMV-Ug strain is now confirmed in Guinea, Sierra Leone and Côte d'Ivoire, with higher infection along the Liberia border (28.85%) than the Guinea border (17.07%), and every cultivated accession tested is susceptible.

Cassava mosaic disease is advancing eastward across West Africa: the pandemic EACMV-Ug strain is now confirmed in Guinea, Sierra Leone and Côte d'Ivoire, with higher infection along the Liberia border (28.85%) than the Guinea border (17.07%), and every cultivated accession tested is susceptible.

Whitefly-mediated transmission is rising (50.6% of spread in 2023), while improved varieties reduce CMD incidence by roughly 30 percentage points - surveillance and clean planting material are the binding constraints.

  • Pandemic spread linked to recombinants and high vector abundance
  • management via diagnostics, phytosanitation and resistant varieties (Adv Virus Res 2015).
  • Awareness campaigns lift CMD knowledge and practice adoption, but CMD persists where healthy planting material is scarce and whiteflies abundant (JAFR 2025).

Why this report exists

Context & Objective

  • The pandemic EACMV-Ug strain (1990s Uganda outbreak lineage) has been detected across Guinea, Sierra Leone and Côte d'Ivoire.

  • improved varieties cut CMD incidence by roughly 30 percentage points.
  • Awareness campaigns improve knowledge but CMD persists because healthy planting material is scarce.

What the findings mean

Discussion

  • The West African front mirrors the dynamics that drove the 1990s East African pandemic - same strain, same vector route, same susceptibility landscape.
  • Improved varieties are the clearest protective lever, but distribution of clean cuttings lags awareness.
  • Whitefly share rising to ~50% means vector monitoring must sit alongside variety deployment.
  • Survey standardisation is the prerequisite for tracking the front across borders.

What changes because of these findings

Decision Implications

  • If clean, improved planting material is not scaled in the border zones, then the eastward front will continue through susceptible local varieties.
  • If whitefly-mediated transmission grows, then phytosanitation and vector monitoring become as important as variety choice.
  • If surveys stay non-standardised, then cross-border tracking of the front remains unreliable.

Immediate · Near-term · Long-term

Recommendations

Immediate

Deploy standardised incidence/severity surveys along the Liberia-Côte d'Ivoire border corridor.|NEAR|Scale distribution of improved, CMD-tolerant varieties with clean planting material in the front zones.|NEAR|Monitor whitefly populations and transmission share alongside CMD surveillance.|LONG|Coordinate regional seed-system and phytosanitary response across West African borders.

Stated plainly

Limitations

Only publicly available literature was analyzed. Findings reflect only the papers screened; absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence. This report verifies citation provenance, not the validity of the underlying studies. Some studies used heterogeneous methodologies, limiting direct comparability. Clinical validation was not performed. It is intended for research and is not a medical device.
