## Executive Summary

- East African bloodstream infections face entrenched resistance: 50-100% of isolates resist first-line antibiotics, ceftriaxone is failing in 46-69% of Gram-negatives, and sustainable NAP implementation ranges from 7% (Burundi) to 94% (Kenya).
- Surveillance capacity is uneven, and gaps in lab capacity, representativeness and financing mask the true burden.

## Key Takeaways

- NAP implementation ranges 7-94% across EAC
- WHO checklist completion 44-100%.
- First-line drug resistance 50-100% (ampicillin, cotrimoxazole), mainly Klebsiella and E. coli

## Findings

### Finding 01
**Evidence:** Progress on sustainable AMR-NAP implementation ranges 7% (Burundi) to 94% (Kenya); WHO AMR-surveillance checklist completion 44-100%; gaps in lab capacity, representativeness, integration and financing
**Source:** Antimicrobial Resistance &amp; Infection Control (2025) 10.1186/s13756-025-01662-y — EAC national public health labs / expert panel (Burundi, Kenya, Rwanda, South Sudan, Tanzania, Uganda)

### Finding 02
**Evidence:** High resistance to first-line drugs 50-100% (ampicillin, cotrimoxazole), emerging ceftriaxone resistance (46-69%) in Gram-negatives, mainly Klebsiella and E. coli; MRSA 2.6-4%
**Source:** African Journal of Laboratory Medicine (2016) 10.4102/ajlm.v5i1.432 — 12 East African studies (2005+, PubMed + AJOL), mostly bloodstream infections

### Finding 03
**Evidence:** Pathogenic bacteria in 14% of patients; first report of community-acquired ESBL Enterobacteriaceae bloodstream infections in Zanzibar; 6/7 Salmonella Typhi isolates multidrug resistant
**Source:** PLOS ONE (2015) 10.1371/journal.pone.0145632 — 469 consecutively enrolled patients, Mnazi Mmoja Hospital, Zanzibar

### Finding 04
**Evidence:** HCAI driven by Klebsiella, S. aureus, E. coli, Pseudomonas; MRSA 3.9-56.8%; ESBL Gram-negatives 1.9-53%; surveillance sparse
**Source:** African Journal of Laboratory Medicine (2018) 10.4102/ajlm.v7i2.796 — Systematic review 2010-2017, 35 African studies (>60% East Africa)

## Discussion

- Resistance to first-line antibiotics is effectively the norm in East African bloodstream isolates, leaving second-line agents exposed.
- The wide NAP implementation range shows surveillance quality varies widely, which can understate true prevalence.
- ESBL and MRSA ranges are broad, reflecting heterogeneous studies and settings more than true uncertainty.
- Surveillance systems are strongest where they sample consistently and publish openly.

## Decision Implications

- If a country under-reports NAP progress, then regional AMR trend estimates are biased and resources misallocated.
- If ceftriaxone resistance is rising, then empiric sepsis protocols relying on it need stewardship review.
- If community ESBLs are spreading, then hospital and community surveillance both warrant expansion.

## Recommendations

- **[IMMEDIATE]** Improve laboratory capacity and core surveillance representativeness in the weakest EAC states.|NEAR|Standardize regional AMR national reporting and align with the WHO surveillance checklist.|NEAR|Secure sustainable financing for national reference laboratories.|LONG|Link clinical data to national surveillance to catch drug-resistance shifts earlier.

## Limitations

- Findings reflect only the papers screened; absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence.
- This report verifies citation provenance, not the validity of the underlying studies.
- Only publicly available literature was analyzed; clinical validation was not performed.
- Some studies had heterogeneous methodologies, limiting direct comparability.
- Screened set: Antimicrobial Resistance &amp; Infection Control (2025) 10.1186/s13756-025-01662-y, African Journal of Laboratory Medicine (2016) 10.4102/ajlm.v5i1.432, PLOS ONE (2015) 10.1371/journal.pone.0145632, African Journal of Laboratory Medicine (2018) 10.4102/ajlm.v7i2.796
