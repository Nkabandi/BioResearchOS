01 — Executive Summary

BIORESEARCH·OS

Research at a glance

AMR-NAP implementation ranges 7% (Burundi) to 94% (Kenya)

WHO surveillance checklist completion 44-100%. Resistance to first-line drugs is 50-100% (ampicillin, cotrimoxazole) with emerging ceftriaxone resistance 46-69% in Gram-negatives. First community-acquired ESBL bloodstream infections reported in Zanzibar

MRSA 3.9-56.8% and ESBL 1.9-53% in healthcare-associated infections.

Research question

“What is the current state of AMR surveillance and bloodstream infection pathogen resistance in East Africa?”

02 — Key Findings

4 verified of 4 screened

What the evidence says

Finding 01

Progress on sustainable AMR-NAP implementation ranges 7% (Burundi) to 94% (Kenya); WHO AMR-surveillance checklist completion 44-100%; gaps in lab capacity, representativeness, integration and financing

Evidence EAC national public health labs / expert panel (Burundi, Kenya, Rwanda, South Sudan, Tanzania, Uganda)

Finding 02

High resistance to first-line drugs 50-100% (ampicillin, cotrimoxazole), emerging ceftriaxone resistance (46-69%) in Gram-negatives, mainly Klebsiella and E. coli; MRSA 2.6-4%

Evidence 12 East African studies (2005+, PubMed + AJOL), mostly bloodstream infections

Finding 03

Pathogenic bacteria in 14% of patients; first report of community-acquired ESBL Enterobacteriaceae bloodstream infections in Zanzibar; 6/7 Salmonella Typhi isolates multidrug resistant

Evidence 469 consecutively enrolled patients, Mnazi Mmoja Hospital, Zanzibar
