# CRISPR-Based Diagnostics for Tuberculosis: Point-of-Care Performance and Clinical Utility

*Verified evidence brief on RPA/CRISPR and cell-free DNA detection of Mycobacterium tuberculosis*

**Client:** BioResearchOS Portfolio   **Report date:** 2026-08-07   **Version:** v1   **References screened:** 6   **Crossref-verified:** **6/6**

## Executive Summary

- CRISPR-based TB diagnostics have reached clinically viable performance for point-of-care use: lab-in-tube RPA+CRISPR-Cas12a detects MTB with 81% sensitivity (14% above GeneXpert) and 94% specificity in under an hour, meeting WHO TPP for non-sputum TB.
- cfDNA-based systems go further: CRISPR-Cas13 approaches reach 96% sensitivity / 94% specificity in adults and 100% in CLHIV.

## Key Takeaways

- Lab-in-tube RPA+CRISPR-Cas12a: 81% sensitivity vs culture (GeneXpert 68%), 94% specificity, <1h, meets WHO TPP for non-sputum TB (Sci Transl Med 2025).
- CRISPR-TB cfDNA: 96%/94% sensitivity/specificity adults
- 83%/95% children
- 100% in CLHIV
- signal clears by 6 months of treatment (Lancet Microbe 2022).
- WATSON pooled genomic tiling + CRISPR/Cas13 detects Mtb-cfDNA with higher sensitivity than singleton targets
- lateral-flow compatible (Nat Commun 2023).
- RPA/CRISPR-Cas12f1 dual-set: LOD 10 copies/µL (fluorescence), 100 copies/µL (lateral flow)
- 94.5-91.4% vs qPCR, 100% specificity, <1h (Microbiol Spectr 2025).
- CADEM: species-level MTB/MAC/MAB cfDNA differentiation, no false positives, ~2h workflow (Anal Chim Acta 2026).

## Context & Objective

- Conventional microscopy, X-ray and culture are slow or insensitive
- point-of-care molecular options are limited.
- CRISPR-based approaches (RPA-Cas12, Cas12f1, Cas13 tiling) promise rapid, specific detection from non-sputum samples.
- Recent assays target both cellular RNA and circulating cell-free DNA (cfDNA) for TB.
- WHO target product profile for non-sputum TB diagnostic is met by several recent systems.

**In scope:**

- CRISPR-based TB detection
- laboratory-in-tube and lateral-flow platforms

## Findings

### Finding 01
**Evidence:** Portable lab-in-tube RPA+CRISPR-Cas12a assay detected MTB DNA with 81% sensitivity vs culture (68% GeneXpert) and 94% specificity, meeting WHO TPP for non-sputum TB; result within 1 h
**Source:** Science Translational Medicine (2025) 10.1126/scitranslmed.adp6411 — Children 1-16y (serum cfDNA) + adults (sputum/saliva)

### Finding 02
**Evidence:** CRISPR-TB: 96% sensitivity / 94% specificity adults; 83%/95% children; 100% in CLHIV; seropositivity positively hazard; signal cleared by 6 months of treatment
**Source:** The Lancet Microbe (2022) 10.1016/S2666-5247(22)00087-8 — Adults, children incl. HIV-positive, Eswatini + Kenya (archived serum)

### Finding 03
**Evidence:** WATSON (pooled genomic tiling + CRISPR/Cas13) detects Mtb-cfDNA with higher sensitivity than singleton target for whole-genome, and compatible with lateral flow tradu in future
**Source:** Nature Communications (2023) 10.1038/s41467-023-37183-8 — Plasma from active pulmonary TB patients (proof-of-concept)

### Finding 04
**Evidence:** RPA/CRISPR-Cas12f1_ge4.1 dual-set fluorescence/lateral flow: LOD 10 copies/µL (FL), 100 (LT); sensitivity vs qPCR 94.5-91.4 with 100% specificity; <1h
**Source:** Microbiology Spectrum (2025) 10.1128/spectrum.02652-24 — Clinical sputum: 73 true positive samples + 40 negative controls

### Finding 05
**Evidence:** Species-level MTB/MAC/MAB differentiation; all mycobacterial positives called correctly, no false positives; 2-hour workflow
**Source:** Analytica Chimica Acta (2026) 10.1016/j.aca.2026.345085 — microfluidic cfDNA enrichment + CRISPR/Cas12a, 20 clinical specimens (7 NTM/MTB, 13 controls)

### Finding 06
**Evidence:** Conventional microscure/X-ray/PPD limitations; molecular (incl. CRISPR-Cas/MiRNA) and nano-approaches improve sensitivity/specificity, argued cost-effective for POC
**Source:** Tuberculosis (2023) 10.1016/j.tube.2023.102340 — Review of TB diagnostics (conventional to molecular/nano)

## Discussion

- The strongest near-term win is non-sputum testing: molecular diagnosis from cell-free DNA or blood bypasses specimen collection barriers.
- Performance is comparable to or better than existing rapid molecular tests in the head-to-head comparisons reported.
- The movement toward lateral-flow readability addresses the biggest deployment constraint in low-resource settings.
- Species-level differentiation (CADEM) hints at multi-pathogen panels on one platform.

## Decision Implications

- If non-sputum CRISPR tests are validated at scale, then sample collection and transport bottlenecks to TB diagnosis shrink.
- If lateral-flow-readable CRISPR tests reach market, then true community-level POC diagnosis becomes realistic.
- If cfDNA signal clears with treatment, then the same assay can double as treatment monitoring.

## Recommendations

- **[IMMEDIATE]** Validate lead CRISPR platforms against culture in high-burden African sites.|NEAR|Field-test non-sputum (blood-based) workflows and lateral-flow usability in clinic settings.|NEAR|Integrate species-level differentiation (MTB vs MAC/MAB) in panels for CFU-ambiguous patients.|LONG|Pursue WHO endorsement and registry/regulatory pathways with package-design partners.

## Limitations

- Findings reflect only the papers screened; absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence.
- This report verifies citation provenance, not the validity of the underlying studies.
- Only publicly available literature was analyzed; clinical validation was not performed.
- Some studies had heterogeneous methodologies, limiting direct comparability.
