## Executive Summary

- Cassava mosaic disease is advancing eastward across West Africa: the pandemic EACMV-Ug strain is now confirmed in Guinea, Sierra Leone and Côte d'Ivoire, with higher infection along the Liberia border (28.85%) than the Guinea border (17.07%), and every cultivated accession tested is susceptible.
- Whitefly-mediated transmission is rising (50.6% of spread in 2023), while improved varieties reduce CMD incidence by roughly 30 percentage points - surveillance and clean planting material are the binding constraints.

- CMD incidence 26.4% (2022) vs 10.1% (2023)

## Findings

### Finding 01
**Evidence:** EACMV-Ug (Uganda 1990s pandemic strain) detected across Guinea and Sierra Leone; spread via infected cuttings; all 63 cultivated accessions susceptible; established eastward front developing in West Africa
**Source:** Viruses (2024) 10.3390/v16111691 — country-wide survey, Guinea April 2024; + 2022 Kambia (Sierra Leone) samples

### Finding 02
**Evidence:** Standardised survey methods required to map the ongoing CMD pandemic, because whitefly (Bemisia tabaci) dynamics govern epidemic spread
**Source:** Virus Research (2004) 10.1016/j.virusres.2003.12.021 — Review of field survey methods, whitefly IPM across Africa

### Finding 03
**Evidence:** Separateness and superabundant whitefly populations drive CMG/CBSV pandemic evolution; southern diversity and complex interactions need integrated surveillance
**Source:** Current Opinion in Virology (2018) 10.1016/j.coviro.2018.08.016 — Review of whitefly-transmitted viruses (CMG & CBSV), endosymbiont work

### Finding 04
**Evidence:** Cassava mosaic disease caused by begomoviruses; pandemic spread linked to recombinates and high vector abundance; management via diagnostics, phytosanitation, breeding resistant or immune varieties
**Source:** Advances in Virus Research (2015) 10.1016/bs.aivir.2014.10.001 — Comprehensive review of cassava virus biology, epidemiology, management

### Finding 05
**Evidence:** CMD incidence 26.4% (2022) vs 10.1% (2023); whitefly-mediated transmission share rose to 50.6% in 2023; improved varieties had far lower CMD (~-30 pt) than locals
**Source:** Viruses (2024) 10.3390/v16091469 — Field surveys 2022-2023 in six western Kenya counties; 29 varieties recorded

### Finding 06
**Evidence:** EACMV-Ug confirmed in Côte d'Ivoire for the first time; higher infection along Liberia border (28.85%) than Guinea border (17.07%); all varieties susceptible; cuttings main route
**Source:** Viruses (2026) 10.3390/v18030319 — Côte d'Ivoire border surveys 2022 + 2025 (west border)

### Finding 07
**Evidence:** Awareness campaigns increased CMD knowledge and adoption of practices but CMD still present in majority of fields because healthy planting material/scarcity or whiteflies in surplus
**Source:** Journal of Agriculture and Food Research (2025) 10.1016/j.jafr.2025.101827 — 305 farmers + 77 fields, Benin; PSM analysis

## Discussion

- The West African front mirrors the dynamics that drove the 1990s East African pandemic - same strain, same vector route, same susceptibility landscape.
- Improved varieties are the clearest protective lever, but distribution of clean cuttings lags awareness.
- Whitefly share rising to ~50% means vector monitoring must sit alongside variety deployment.
- Survey standardisation is the prerequisite for tracking the front across borders.

## Decision Implications

- If clean, improved planting material is not scaled in the border zones, then the eastward front will continue through susceptible local varieties.
- If whitefly-mediated transmission grows, then phytosanitation and vector monitoring become as important as variety choice.
- If surveys stay non-standardised, then cross-border tracking of the front remains unreliable.

## Recommendations

- **[IMMEDIATE]** Deploy standardised incidence/severity surveys along the Liberia-Côte d'Ivoire border corridor.|NEAR|Scale distribution of improved, CMD-tolerant varieties with clean planting material in the front zones.|NEAR|Monitor whitefly populations and transmission share alongside CMD surveillance.|LONG|Coordinate regional seed-system and phytosanitary response across West African borders.

## Limitations

- Findings reflect only the papers screened; absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence.
- This report verifies citation provenance, not the validity of the underlying studies.
- Only publicly available literature was analyzed; clinical validation was not performed.
- Some studies had heterogeneous methodologies, limiting direct comparability.
- Screened set: Viruses (2024) 10.3390/v16111691, Virus Research (2004) 10.1016/j.virusres.2003.12.021, Current Opinion in Virology (2018) 10.1016/j.coviro.2018.08.016, Advances in Virus Research (2015) 10.1016/bs.aivir.2014.10.001…
