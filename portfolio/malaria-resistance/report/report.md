## Executive Summary

- Artemisinin partial resistance is established in East Africa: pooled non-synonymous PfKelch13 prevalence is 5.0% (CI 3.0-7.0%), validated markers R561H and A675V dominate, and Africa is tracking the Southeast Asia pattern 10-15 years earlier.
- ACTs remain effective, but partner-drug tolerance (lumefantrine) and high HRP2/3 deletions demand continual genomic surveillance.

## Findings

### Finding 01
**Evidence:** ART-R has emerged in multiple East African countries and is now a priority; recommended expansion of genomic surveillance, clinical efficacy trials, ex-vivo susceptibility testing, policy adaptation
**Source:** The Lancet Infectious Diseases (2024) 10.1016/S1473-3099(24)00141-5 — Multi-institution review incl. WHO (Rossert HQ colleagues, Uganda/Tanzania/Rwanda)

### Finding 02
**Evidence:** 7.0% non-synonymous Pfk13 mutations incl. WHO-validated R561H (2 patients, 2022); pfmdr1 N86 invariable; mean MOI 1.5; suggests emerging lumefantrine tolerance
**Source:** Malaria Journal (2025) 10.1186/s12936-025-05447-x — 173 P. falciparum-positive children, Mwanza, Tanzania (2016-2022), 143 Pfk13 sequenced

### Finding 03
**Evidence:** Pooled non-synonymous PfKelch13 prevalence 5.0% (Cl 3.0-7.0%); R561H and A675V most common; mutations associated with higher risk of treatment failure (log OR -2.06)
**Source:** PLOS One (2026) 10.1371/journal.pone.0354429 — Systematic review + meta-analysis of 24 studies (2014-2024), East Africa

### Finding 04
**Evidence:** Five validated pfk13 ART-R markers in Africa: R561H (Rwanda, Tanzania), M476I (Tanzania), C580Y (Ghana), F446I (Mali), P553L (Angola); distribution central to East Africa
**Source:** Malaria Journal (2021) 10.1186/s12936-021-03987-6 — 87 studies, 37,864 isolates across 29 African countries

### Finding 05
**Evidence:** Validated ART-R marker pfk13 R622I in ~2/174 (1.1%); pfmdr1 pattern consistent with reduced lumen sensitivity; high HRP2/3-deletion prevalence (HRP2 del 24%, HRP3 del 79%, dual 22.7%)
**Source:** The American Journal of Tropical Medicine and Hygiene (2025) 10.4269/ajtmh.25-0114 — Symptomatic malaria patients at primary centres, central Ethiopia, 2023 (220 isolates)

### Finding 06
**Evidence:** ART-R in Africa follows the Southeast Asia pattern 10-15 years earlier; kelch13 data aggregated into a single surveillance resource with focus on East Africa
**Source:** eLife (2025) 10.7554/eLife.105544 — Aggregation of 112,933 P. falciparum samples (1980-2023), review

### Finding 07
**Evidence:** ART resistance is mediated by kelch13; ACTs remain effective but partner-drug vulnerabilities and regional spread of markers demand continual monitoring
**Source:** Current Opinion in Microbiology (2022) 10.1016/j.mib.2022.102193 — Review of artemisinin-based combination therapy resistance (Pfk13, partner drugs)

## Discussion

- ART-R marker prevalence of ~5% is still low, but the trajectory matters: it mirrors Southeast Asia's earlier timeline.
- The log-OR -2.06 association with treatment failure shows markers are clinically meaningful, not just molecular curiosities.
- Partner-drug tolerance (lumefantrine) is emerging while ACTs remain effective overall - the vulnerable window is now.
- HRP2/3 deletions at these levels would degrade RDT-only diagnosis strategies.

## Decision Implications

- If surveillance is not expanded now, then marker spread will be detected only after treatment failure rises.
- If RDT reliance continues without HRP2/3 deletion monitoring, then false-negative diagnosis risk grows.
- If partner-drug tolerance spreads, then ACT policy adaptation will be needed within a treatment window that is closing.

## Recommendations

- **[IMMEDIATE]** Expand genomic surveillance of PfKelch13 markers across East African sites.|NEAR|Run clinical efficacy trials and ex-vivo susceptibility testing alongside molecular surveillance.|NEAR|Monitor HRP2/3 deletions wherever RDTs are the primary diagnostic.|LONG|Adapt ACT policy as marker prevalence and partner-drug tolerance data accumulate.

- This report verifies citation provenance, not the validity of the underlying studies.
- Only publicly available literature was analyzed; clinical validation was not performed.
