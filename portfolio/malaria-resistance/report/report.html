01 — Executive Summary

BIORESEARCH·OS

Research at a glance

Pooled non-synonymous PfKelch13 prevalence is 5.0% (95% CI 3.0-7.0%) in East Africa

R561H and A675V are the most common validated markers

R622I is emerging in central Ethiopia (1.1%)

HRP2/HRP3 gene deletions are common (HRP2 del 24%, HRP3 del 79%). ART-R in Africa follows the Southeast Asian pattern 10-15 years earlier

clinical efficacy monitoring of ACTs is required.

Research question

“What molecular evidence supports the ongoing spread of artemisinin partial resistance in East Africa Plasmodium falciparum?”

02 — Key Findings

7 verified of 7 screened

What the evidence says

Finding 01

ART-R has emerged in multiple East African countries and is now a priority; recommended expansion of genomic surveillance, clinical efficacy trials, ex-vivo susceptibility testing, policy adaptation

Evidence Multi-institution review incl. WHO (Rossert HQ colleagues, Uganda/Tanzania/Rwanda)

Finding 02

7.0% non-synonymous Pfk13 mutations incl. WHO-validated R561H (2 patients, 2022); pfmdr1 N86 invariable; mean MOI 1.5; suggests emerging lumefantrine tolerance

Evidence 173 P. falciparum-positive children, Mwanza, Tanzania (2016-2022), 143 Pfk13 sequenced

Finding 03

Pooled non-synonymous PfKelch13 prevalence 5.0% (Cl 3.0-7.0%); R561H and A675V most common; mutations associated with higher risk of treatment failure (log OR -2.06)

Evidence Systematic review + meta-analysis of 24 studies (2014-2024), East Africa
