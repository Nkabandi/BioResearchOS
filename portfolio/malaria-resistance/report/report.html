Executive Summary

Artemisinin partial resistance is established in East Africa: pooled non-synonymous PfKelch13 prevalence is 5.0% (CI 3.0-7.0%), validated markers R561H and A675V dominate, and Africa is tracking the Southeast Asia pattern 10-15 years earlier.

ACTs remain effective, but partner-drug tolerance (lumefantrine) and high HRP2/3 deletions demand continual genomic surveillance.

  • ART-R is a regional priority: multiple East African countries have confirmed markers (Lancet ID 2024).
  • Pooled non-synonymous PfKelch13 prevalence 5.0% (CI 3.0-7.0%)

  • pfmdr1 N86 invariable
  • mean MOI 1.5.

  • pfmdr1 pattern consistent with reduced lumefantrine sensitivity.

  • kelch13 data now aggregated in a single surveillance resource.

Why this report exists

Context & Objective

  • Artemisinin partial resistance (ART-R) has emerged in multiple East African countries and is now a regional priority.
  • Five validated PfKelch13 markers are present in Africa, with distribution centred on East Africa (R561H Rwanda/Tanzania, M476I Tanzania).
  • Africa appears to be following the Southeast Asia resistance pattern 10-15 years earlier.
  • HRP2/3 deletions threaten rapid-diagnostic-test reliance in the region.

What the findings mean

Discussion

  • ART-R marker prevalence of ~5% is still low, but the trajectory matters: it mirrors Southeast Asia's earlier timeline.
  • The log-OR -2.06 association with treatment failure shows markers are clinically meaningful, not just molecular curiosities.
  • Partner-drug tolerance (lumefantrine) is emerging while ACTs remain effective overall - the vulnerable window is now.
  • HRP2/3 deletions at these levels would degrade RDT-only diagnosis strategies.

What changes because of these findings

Decision Implications

  • If surveillance is not expanded now, then marker spread will be detected only after treatment failure rises.
  • If RDT reliance continues without HRP2/3 deletion monitoring, then false-negative diagnosis risk grows.
  • If partner-drug tolerance spreads, then ACT policy adaptation will be needed within a treatment window that is closing.

Immediate · Near-term · Long-term

Recommendations

Immediate

Expand genomic surveillance of PfKelch13 markers across East African sites.|NEAR|Run clinical efficacy trials and ex-vivo susceptibility testing alongside molecular surveillance.|NEAR|Monitor HRP2/3 deletions wherever RDTs are the primary diagnostic.|LONG|Adapt ACT policy as marker prevalence and partner-drug tolerance data accumulate.

Limitations

Only publicly available literature was analyzed. Findings reflect only the papers screened; absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence. This report verifies citation provenance, not the validity of the underlying studies. Some studies used heterogeneous methodologies, limiting direct comparability. Clinical validation was not performed. It is intended for research and is not a medical device.
